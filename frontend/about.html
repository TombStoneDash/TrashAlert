Making trash day obvious.

TrashAlert started with a simple frustration: nobody should have to Google when their trash gets picked up.

In 2025, we started mapping pickup zones for six cities in California's Imperial Valley. We geocoded addresses, built an H3-tessellated zone map, and launched a simple lookup API. Property managers noticed. Tenants stopped calling.

Today we cover 26+ cities across the country, with more onboarding every week. Our data comes from municipal open data portals, waste hauler APIs, and thousands of crowdsourced community reports verified through our consensus algorithm.

What we build

For property managers: Embeddable widgets, bulk schedule APIs, and tenant notification tools that eliminate "what day is trash?" calls.

For municipalities: Interactive zone maps, resident notification systems, and analytics dashboards that reduce 311 call volume.

For residents: A free lookup tool — type your address, see your schedule. No app download, no account required.

Our values

Data accuracy first

Every schedule is verified. Our consensus algorithm requires 3+ independent reports with 67%+ agreement before marking data as verified.

Open by default

Address lookups are free for residents. Our API has generous free tiers. Pickup schedules should be public infrastructure.

Build for the edge case

Holiday schedules, route changes, bulk pickup weeks — the exceptions are where the real value is.

Who we are

TrashAlert is built by Hudson Taylor at Tombstone Dash LLC in San Diego, California. What started as a side project to map trash zones in the Imperial Valley has grown into a data platform serving property managers and residents in 26+ cities.

The team is lean by design: one founder, a fleet of AI agents, and a growing network of municipal data partnerships. We ship fast and prioritize data accuracy over feature bloat.
